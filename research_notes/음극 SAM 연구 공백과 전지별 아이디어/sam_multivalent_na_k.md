# SAMs and molecular monolayers on multivalent (Mg, Ca, Al) and beyond-Li alkali (Na, K) metal anodes and their current collectors — survey and gap map (2012–2026)

**Method / provenance note.** All findings below come from web-search result snippets and abstracts (WebSearch, 2026-10-31). Full-text fetches were impossible in this session: pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov, pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov, pubs.acs.org, onlinelibrary.wiley.com, advanced.onlinelibrary.wiley.com, www.sciencedirect.com, www.nature.com, www.cell.com, www.osti.gov, www.researchgate.net, api.semanticscholar.org and ora.ox.ac.uk are all outside the allowed network domains, and the per-turn web-search budget was exhausted before a final verification sweep. Therefore: (i) every metric is as quoted in a search snippet, not checked against the paper; (ii) items marked **[unverified]** have a plausible but unconfirmed detail (journal, year, number); (iii) items marked **[absence]** are explicit null results — repeated, differently-phrased searches returned nothing on-topic. Absence of literature is a central finding of this survey.

**Scope reminder.** Li, Zn, Si are covered elsewhere; Zn/Li SAM papers appear here only as the closest transferable chemistry and are flagged as analogues.

## KQ1. Which SAM molecules have been applied to Mg, Ca, Al, Na, K metal anodes or their current collectors, and with what results?

### Takeaway
Across the five metals, only **one** peer-reviewed study explicitly frames itself as a self-assembled monolayer on a battery anode: an in-situ silane-derived SAM on Mg (Fan et al., Angew. Chem. 2025, TFTMS). Everything else that comes close is (a) a small-molecule electrolyte additive that adsorbs/decomposes into a thin interphase (Mg: TMSB, BI3, o-fluoroaniline, PrIm), (b) a non-monolayer artificial interphase (alloy/halide/polymer/MOF/COF layers), or (c) a corrosion-protection SAM on Mg or Al alloys with no electrochemical plating/stripping test. For Ca, Al (non-aqueous and aqueous), Na and K metal anodes, and for Mg/Ca/Na/K current collectors, repeated searches found **no SAM study at all**.

### Cited Findings

#### Mg metal anode — the one explicit SAM study
- **Fan et al., "Regulating Electric Double Layer via Self-Assembled Monolayer for Stable Solid/Electrolyte Interphase on Mg Metal Anode", Angew. Chem. Int. Ed. 2025, DOI 10.1002/anie.202416582.** Molecule: (trifluoromethyl)trimethylsilane (TFTMS), added at ~2 vol% to the electrolyte (in-situ SAM, not an ex-situ pre-formed coating). Substrate: Mg metal. Electrolyte: Mg(TFSI)2-based (search snippet; exact solvent **[unverified]**). Mechanism: electron-withdrawing CF3 groups bind to the Mg surface and reshape the electric double layer; F···H dipole interactions with solvent weaken solvent–Mg2+ coordination, pushing anions into the interphase and giving a dense, electronically insulating SEI that supports horizontal Mg plating/stripping. Metric: symmetric-cell cycling up to 1000 h at 5 mA cm-2; authors state the approach transfers to other electrolyte systems. CE and overpotential values were not in the snippets **[unverified]**. — [Wiley](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1002/anie.202416582); [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39471338/)
- It is already cited by a 2026 Angew. paper on high-rate Mg metal batteries ("Solvent–Anion Counterpoised Electrolyte", Zhang et al., 2026), indicating follow-on uptake. — [Wiley](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/anie.2598559)

### Inferences
- Of all search hits, the Mg-TFTMS paper is the only one that actually self-labels as a SAM for a Mg/Ca/Al/Na/K anode; the "SAM" literature on these metals is effectively n = 1 (plus silane-additive relatives), versus dozens of Zn and Li SAM papers.
- The field's de-facto substitute for SAMs on multivalent anodes is "adsorbing additive → in-situ thin interphase" (TFTMS, TMSB, BI3, o-fluoroaniline), which blurs the SAM/SEI boundary and leaves the ex-situ, pre-formed, well-ordered monolayer essentially untested.
- Phosphonic-acid and carboxylate headgroups are proven to bind Mg-oxide/hydroxide and Al-oxide surfaces (corrosion literature), so the chemistry to make SAMs on native-oxide-covered Mg and Al already exists; it has simply not been carried into electrochemical plating/stripping tests.

### Gaps
- Full text of Fan et al. 2025 could not be read: CE, exact electrolyte formulation, areal capacity, and whether a Cl-containing electrolyte was among the "other electrolyte systems" remain unverified.
- The claimed Al-anode COF "anion-concentrating" coating could not be traced to a primary citation.
- No Na, K, Ca, or Al SAM paper could be found; it is possible that one exists under non-SAM terminology (e.g., "molecular layer", "organic monolayer", "surface grafting"), but four to five rephrasings per metal returned nothing.

## KQ2. Is there any demonstration of SAM-enabled plating/stripping of Ca or Al in otherwise passivating electrolytes?

### Takeaway
No. There is no published demonstration of a SAM (or any ex-situ molecular monolayer) enabling Ca or Al plating/stripping in a passivating electrolyte. The only ways Ca has been made reversible in Ca(TFSI)2-type or carbonate electrolytes are electrolyte-derived borate-rich interphases, pre-passivation in a borate electrolyte, alloy/halide artificial layers, or solvation-structure control; for Al, no coating-based route in chloroaluminate or aqueous electrolytes uses a monolayer.

### Cited Findings
- Carbonate-based passivation layers formed in Ca(TFSI)2 electrolytes block Ca plating/stripping; a pre-passivation procedure in a borate electrolyte makes plating in Ca(TFSI)2 possible, with faster kinetics attributed to absence of contact ion pairs (Forero-Saboya et al., EES 2020). — [ALBA](https://www.cells.es/en/public/news/borate-based-passivation-layers-enable-reversible-calcium-batteries)
- Boron moieties appear crucial for Ca2+ transport; a boron-free control produced a blocking layer. — [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/343899826_Understanding_the_nature_of_the_passivation_layer_enabling_reversible_calcium_plating)
- Reversible RT Ca plating/stripping with Ca[B(hfip)4]2 in DME, CaF2 from anion reduction in the interphase (ACS Energy Lett. 2019). — [ACS](https://pubs.acs.org/doi/10.1021/acsenergylett.9b01550)
- Ca31Sn20/CaBr2 artificial layer: low polarization −25 to +50 °C (ACS Energy Lett. 2023). — [ACS](https://pubs.acs.org/doi/abs/10.1021/acsenergylett.2c02366)
- Ca(BH4)2/THF leaves a CaH2 passivation layer after cycling (summary in 2026 AFM review). — [Wiley](https://advanced.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/adfm.202600035)
- A 2026 Joule review states that reversible Ca plating in Ca(TFSI)2 is linked to a borate-rich interphase and that the field is "still far from plating/stripping efficiencies of over 99.9%". — [Cell/Joule](https://www.cell.com/joule/fulltext/S2542-4351(26)00085-1?rss=yes)
- Al in chloroaluminate: the oxide film is "partially etched while an inhomogeneous passivation layer forms" during pretreatment; dendrite and corrosion are the main anode failure modes. — [Wiley](https://advanced.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1002/admi.202500289?af=R); [PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13486223/)
- Aqueous Al anodes: HER raises pH and feeds passivation; protective layers work by "covering the hydrogen evolution sites". — [PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12464664/); [PMC](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC10582464/)
- Computationally, Young & Smeu (Adv. Theory Simul. 2021, 4, 2100018) show an amorphous Al2O3 artificial SEI on Ca intercalates Ca up to ~Ca1.5Al2O3, stays insulating, and "completely prevents the decomposition of solvent molecules" in AIMD with a realistic organic electrolyte — the only artificial-interphase-on-Ca modeling found; not a SAM. — [NJIT](https://digitalcommons.njit.edu/fac_pubs/3910); [OSTI](https://www.osti.gov/pages/biblio/1807591-preventing-electrolyte-decomposition-ca-metal-electrode-interface-using-artificial-solidelectrolyte-interphase)

### Inferences
- The borate-moiety finding suggests a design hypothesis for a Ca SAM: a boronate/borate-terminated monolayer (e.g., aryl-boronic-acid or B(hfip)-type tail) might emulate the Ca2+-permeable borate interphase ex situ — nothing in the literature tests this.
- For Al in Lewis-acidic chloroaluminates, any SAM must survive AlCl4-/Al2Cl7- attack on the headgroup; the fact that immersion alone etches the oxide implies oxide-anchored phosphonate SAMs would likely be stripped — unstudied.

### Gaps
- No Ca or Al SAM electrochemistry exists to cite; the question is answered only by absence.
- Could not verify whether any Ca "pre-passivation" step used an organic molecule (as opposed to the borate electrolyte) — full texts inaccessible.

## KQ3. What is known about SAM compatibility with Mg electrolytes (Mg(TFSI)2/DME, MgCl2–AlCl3/APC-type, Mg(CB11H12)2)?

### Takeaway
Only Mg(TFSI)2/DME-family and Mg[B(hfip)4]2/DME have any monolayer/silane data (Fan 2025; Chandrappa 2026). No SAM study was found in chloride-rich electrolytes (MgCl2–AlCl3, APC, MACC) or in carborane Mg(CB11H12)2 electrolytes. Headgroup compatibility therefore rests on indirect evidence: the native Mg(TFSI)2 passivation chemistry (Mg(OH)2/MgS/MgO), known thiol instability on Cu-like metals, and known phosphonate/carboxylate binding to Mg-oxide.

### Cited Findings
- Reductive decomposition of Mg(TFSI)2 forms a passivation layer of Mg(OH)2, MgS and MgO, causing large stripping overpotential; DME in the primary solvation shell co-reduces with Mg2+ to form electron-conducting, Mg2+-blocking layers. — [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S138589472503236X); [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0021979725018508)
- Decomposition of dissociated TFSI- remains a challenge; magnesiophilic N/O-rich groups lower nucleation barriers (Xu et al., Adv. Mater. 2026, DOI 10.1002/adma.202514224). — [Wiley](https://advanced.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/adma.202514224)
- TFTMS SAM operates in a Mg(TFSI)2-based electrolyte and is claimed to extend to other systems (unspecified in snippets). — [Wiley](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1002/anie.202416582)
- TMSB silane additive operates in Cl-free Mg[B(hfip)4]2/DME. — [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41144799/)
- Thiolate SAM oxidative stability increases Cu < Pt < Au (ChemRxiv 2022, DOI 10.26434/chemrxiv-2022-dt7dk); alkanethiols on Cu desorb at 150–160 °C with thiolate → sulfonate oxidation. — [ChemRxiv](https://chemrxiv.org/doi/pdf/10.26434/chemrxiv-2022-dt7dk); [NSF PAR](https://par.nsf.gov/servlets/purl/10433337); [PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13421965/)
- Phosphonic acids bind strongly to metal oxides and give more uniform SAMs than thiols on ZnO; phosphonic-acid SAMs form on Mg-alloy and Al-oxide surfaces (corrosion studies). — [ACS](https://pubs.acs.org/doi/full/10.1021/jp906013r); [PMC](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12525536/); [MDPI](https://www.mdpi.com/1420-3049/29/3/706)
- Ion-recognition approach for fast Mg–Cl bond dissociation (J. Magnesium Alloys 2023) indicates the Cl-rich interfacial chemistry a SAM would have to coexist with. — [SciOpen](https://www.sciopen.com/local/article_pdf/10.1016/j.jma.2023.09.021.pdf)

### Inferences
- In Mg(TFSI)2/DME the operative problem is electron leakage through a Mg2+-blocking film; a SAM's value is as an electron-insulating, solvent-excluding layer (exactly the EDL-reshaping framing of Fan 2025). In Cl-rich electrolytes, by contrast, the Mg surface is kept bare by MgCl+ adsorption, so a SAM would compete with Cl- adsorption — an untested regime.
- Thiols are a poor headgroup choice for Mg/Ca/Al (no soft-metal affinity, oxidatively fragile, and MgS is itself a passivating product); phosphonates/carboxylates/silanes that bind the native oxide/hydroxide are the realistic candidates, consistent with the only two monolayer-forming Mg additives both being silanes.

### Gaps
- Zero data on SAMs in MgCl2–AlCl3, APC, MACC, or Mg(CB11H12)2 electrolytes — the question cannot be answered from literature.
- Whether TFTMS/TMSB layers survive Cl- attack or Grignard-type nucleophiles is unknown.

## KQ4. Any computational work on SAM–Mg/Ca interfaces or divalent-ion transport through monolayers?

### Takeaway
None found for SAMs specifically. The modeling literature covers Mg SEI formation by AIMD, Mg2+ transport through inorganic (BiOCl) and MOF frameworks, and an amorphous Al2O3 artificial SEI on Ca; the only MD treatment of a SAM-passivated battery electrode is a patent example for fluoride-ion batteries.

### Cited Findings
- AIMD of spontaneous SEI formation at the Mg metal surface (2021; authors not captured). — [OSTI](https://www.osti.gov/pages/biblio/1819510-insights-spontaneous-solid-electrolyte-interphase-formation-magnesium-metal-anode-surface-from-ab-initio-molecular-dynamics-simulations)
- AIMD/LOTF-MD of Mg diffusion in a BiOCl artificial interphase: cooperative Mg–Cl motion. — [OSTI](https://www.osti.gov/servlets/purl/1905892)
- DFT: Mg2+ in Mg-MOF-74 moves by solvent-hopping as the lowest-energy pathway (ACS Appl. Mater. Interfaces 2021, DOI 10.1021/acsami.1c08392). — [ACS](https://pubs.acs.org/doi/abs/10.1021/acsami.1c08392)
- Classical MD: Mg2+ electrolytes less conductive than Li+/Na+ owing to higher polarizing power. — [LIDSEN](https://www.lidsen.com/journals/jept/jept-03-03-043)
- Young & Smeu, Adv. Theory Simul. 2021: DFT + AIMD of amorphous Al2O3 artificial SEI on Ca (see KQ2). — [NJIT](https://digitalcommons.njit.edu/fac_pubs/3910)
- Patent US 11,621,438: all-atom MD of SAMs passivating metal electrodes in fluoride-ion batteries, with SAM coverage and electrode polarization as design variables. — [USPTO](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/11621438)
- Combined DFT/MD protocols for SAM-controlled thin-film morphology exist outside batteries (Langmuir). — [ACS](https://pubs.acs.org/langd5/article-abstract/31/39/10693/1376515/)

### Inferences
- The question "can a SAM be Mg2+-permeable?" has no computational answer in the literature; the MOF-74 solvent-hopping result suggests that a monolayer with periodic O/N donor sites spaced to allow hopping of partially desolvated Mg2+ is the natural hypothesis to simulate.

### Gaps
- No DFT/AIMD/classical-MD study of Mg2+ or Ca2+ crossing, or desolvating at, an organic SAM was found; no study of SAM headgroup binding energies on Mg(0001)/Ca vs. MgO/CaO under electrochemical bias.

## KQ5. What do recent reviews on multivalent (and Na/K) anodes list as open interfacial questions?

### Takeaway
Reviews converge on: the Mg/Ca interphase must be electronically insulating yet cation-permeable; interphase chemistry is set by solvation structure/anion decomposition; practical CE targets (>99.9%) are unmet; Cl-free electrolytes remain passivating; and nanoscale/operando characterization is only now emerging (cryo-EM Mg deposition 2025, dynamic Ca interface EES 2024). None of the reviews identified SAMs as an open strategy for Mg/Ca/Al/Na/K.

### Cited Findings
- 2025 Mg artificial-interphase review: halide-rich (MgCl2/MgF2/metal-halide) interphases are among the most effective inorganic strategies; design space listed as halide, alloy, polymer, MOF/COF, phytic-acid crosslinked layers. — [PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13528967/)
- Adv. Mater. 2026 electrolyte-design review: TFSI- decomposition passivating Mg remains a challenge; atomistic structure–performance links are open. — [Wiley](https://advanced.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/adma.202514224)
- AFM 2026 (Lin et al.) Mg/Ca interfacial-chemistry review: organic-rich interphases in IL + Ca(BH4)2 cause passivation and higher overpotential, versus borate-rich ones. — [Wiley](https://advanced.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/adfm.202600035)
- Joule 2026 Ca review: >99.9% CE required for practical metal anodes is far off. — [Cell/Joule](https://www.cell.com/joule/fulltext/S2542-4351(26)00085-1?rss=yes)
- EES 2024 "Deciphering the dynamic interfacial chemistry of calcium metal anodes" (d4ee01257g) — interphase evolves dynamically during cycling. — [RSC](https://pubs.rsc.org/en/content/articlelanding/2024/ee/d4ee01257g)
- Cryogenic nanoscale visualization of intrinsic Mg deposition (Nat. Commun. 2025, DOI 10.1038/s41467-025-67029-4) — new cryo-EM window on Mg morphology/SEI. — [Nature](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-025-67029-4)
- Joule 2019 "Anode-Electrolyte Interfaces in Secondary Magnesium Batteries" (Attias/Aurbach group) — foundational framing of passivation vs. Cl-mediated bare-surface regime (contents not accessible). — [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2542435118305191)
- Materials Today 2025 review of Al-metal anode challenges (dendrite, passivation, corrosion). — [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S1369702125002652)
- Review of artificial SEI membranes for Li/Na/K: "organic artificial SEI layers may be more effective than the native inorganic–organic SEIs". — [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/344545725_Review_of_Emerging_Concepts_in_SEI_Analysis_and_Artificial_SEI_Membranes_for_Lithium_Sodium_and_Potassium_Metal_Battery_Anodes)
- K substrate-design review: substrate chemistry sets SEI organic fraction; some substrates intensify electrolyte decomposition. — [PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12606554/)
- JACS 2021 SAM perspective (Yi, Mao, Shen, Chen): framed as the first perspective devoted to SAMs in batteries; Na/K mentioned only as prospects. — [Reviewer.ly](https://biomed.reviewer.ly/app/publication/5f593c6e-eab4-4edf-ae7b-84d71c35693c)

### Inferences — consolidated gap map (the deliverable)
1. **Metals with essentially NO SAM literature:** Ca (none), Al non-aqueous (none), Al aqueous/Al–air (only an n-octyl-phosphonic-acid *electrolyte additive*), Na (none; one prospective sentence), K (none). Mg has one in-situ silane SAM (TFTMS) plus one silane additive (TMSB); zero ex-situ pre-formed SAMs on Mg tested electrochemically; zero SAMs on Mg/Ca/Na/K current collectors for anode-free cells.
2. **Headgroup compatibility (inferred, untested):** oxide-anchoring phosphonates/carboxylates/silanes are the only headgroups with demonstrated binding to native Mg/Al oxide; thiols are oxidatively fragile and chemically mismatched (MgS is a passivation product). No headgroup has been tested in Cl-rich (APC/MACC) or carborane electrolytes, nor against Lewis-acidic chloroaluminates.
3. **SAM vs. native passivation film:** every Mg/Ca SAM must be formed on or through a pre-existing MgO/Mg(OH)2 or CaO/CaCO3/CaH2 layer; nobody has reported whether the SAM sits on the oxide (adding a second barrier) or replaces it (after etching/Cl- activation).
4. **Mg2+/Ca2+ permeability of a monolayer:** no experimental or computational answer; the borate-moiety (Ca) and MOF solvent-hopping (Mg) results point to boron/O-donor-rich tails as first hypotheses.
5. **Modeling:** no SAM–Mg/Ca interface simulations; no divalent-ion-through-monolayer transport calculations.
6. **Operando/in-situ characterization of SAM-coated Mg/Ca/Na/K anodes:** none; the only SAM mechanism evidence (Fan 2025) is ex-situ/EDL-based. Cryo-EM (Mg 2025) and dynamic-interface EES (Ca 2024) tools exist but have not been applied to monolayer-modified electrodes.

### Gaps
- Review full texts (PMC13528967, AFM 2026, Joule 2026, Joule 2019) could not be read; their explicit "perspectives" sections are summarized from snippets only.
- The web-search budget ran out before confirming: authors of the AIMD Mg-SEI paper (OSTI 1819510); the exact journal/year of the SbBr3, MgCl2@polymer and o-fluoroaniline papers; the Ca(BF4)2 ion-exchange interphase citation; and any Na/K SAM paper hiding under alternative terminology.
